You can open Instagram, tap Following, and scroll for a long time without ever getting a straight answer. The app shows you who you follow. It does not give you a list of the accounts in that group that never followed you back. That missing list is the whole problem, and it is why people end up checking one profile at a time or pasting random tools into their browser.

This guide is the practical version. It covers what "doesn't follow you back" actually means, how to check a small account by hand, how Instagram's own data download fits in, and how to run the check in the browser with [Unfolks](/), a free side-panel extension for Chrome, Edge, and Brave. It does not ask for your password. It uses the Instagram session already open in your browser, and the lists stay on your computer.

If your real goal is to unfollow a lot of those accounts afterwards, read [how to mass unfollow on Instagram safely](/blog/how-to-mass-unfollow-on-instagram-safely) before you start tapping. Finding the list and clearing it are different jobs, and the second one is the one that can get an account limited.

## What you are actually looking for

"Doesn't follow you back" is a comparison, not a notification. Take the accounts you follow. Remove the ones that also follow you. What is left is the list. It includes brands you followed on purpose, creators you like, private accounts that never accepted a follow, and people who followed you once and later left. Those are not the same kind of account, which is why a raw list is a starting point and not a to-do list.

It is also not the same question as "who unfollowed me". Someone who never followed you is a non-follower. Someone who used to follow you and stopped is an unfollow. Instagram does not send a message when either thing happens. If you want the second question, the method is different, and it is covered in [how to check who unfollowed you on Instagram](/blog/how-to-check-who-unfollowed-you-on-instagram).

The list will not tell you whether an account is inactive, a person who is simply not interested, or a brand you followed on purpose. It also will not unfollow anyone. That part is a separate action, and Instagram can limit it.

## Check by hand, if the account is small

On a following list of thirty or forty people, you do not need a tool. The manual check is slow, and it is the option with the least machinery.

On your phone, open your profile, tap Following, and look at each row. Accounts that follow you are marked. Everyone else on that list does not follow you back. The badge wording moves as Instagram redesigns the app, so trust the mark on people who follow you, and treat unmarked rows as the ones that do not. On instagram.com it is the same idea. There is still no filter for "only the accounts that don't follow me back".

This falls apart once the list is in the hundreds. You lose your place, you skip people, and you have nothing to sort. That is when a file or an extension earns its keep. The manual answer is not wrong. You just will not finish it.

## Use the file Instagram already lets you download

Instagram has a "Download your information" export. You request your followers and following, wait for the file, and compare the two lists on your own computer. During that comparison, nothing new has to be asked of Instagram, which is why this is the check to use when you do not want to risk a limit just to look.

The menus move, so follow the label "Download your information" rather than an old screenshot, and ask for followers and following if Instagram lets you choose a partial export. The step-by-step is in [find non-followers with Instagram's data download](/blog/find-non-followers-with-instagram-data-download). The short version: the file is a snapshot. It will not unfollow anyone, and it will not update itself tomorrow.

## What to do with the names

A long list feels like a chore you should finish in one sitting. That is the habit that gets accounts limited. Instagram does not publish the exact number of unfollows it will allow, and the number is not the same for every account. No tool, including this one, can promise you will not be restricted. Going slowly is the part you control.

Hide who you want to keep, unfollow in a batch that waits, and stop when Instagram stops you. Unfolks waits 4–6 seconds between unfollows by default and rests for 3 minutes after every 5. Both timings can be changed in Settings. Slowing them further is the safer direction. If Instagram starts refusing, the extension stops and shows when you can try again. Posts that quote a precise daily cap are guessing, which the [limits guide](/blog/instagram-follow-and-unfollow-limits) explains. Seeing the list and doing nothing with it is also a valid result.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Instagram show who doesn't follow you back?

Not as a list. Your Following list marks accounts that follow you, so you can check one by one. There is no built-in view of "only the accounts I follow that don't follow me".

### Can I see this without giving anyone my password?

Yes. The manual check uses the app you are already signed in to. The data download comes from Instagram's own export. Unfolks uses the browser session already open on instagram.com. You should not type your Instagram password into a third-party website to answer this question.

### Will looking get my account restricted?

Reading your own lists is ordinary Instagram use, but a fast automated scan can still be limited, because it requests a lot of pages in a row. The data-file check avoids that: the comparison happens on your computer and sends Instagram no requests. Unfollowing is the action most likely to be limited, and no tool can guarantee it will not happen.

### Does Unfolks work on the Instagram app?

No. It is a desktop side panel for Chrome, Edge, and Brave. The phone app is where the manual check lives. Your data download can be requested from the phone and then opened on a computer.

Instagram does not send a notification when someone unfollows you. There is no inbox for it, no email, and no tab labeled "recently lost". If a website promises the complete history of everyone who left your account since the day you joined, it is claiming something Instagram does not give out. You can still answer a narrower, more honest question: who follows you now, and how that differs from the last time you looked.

That narrower question is useful. It is also easy to mix up with a different one. This guide separates them, shows the two snapshots that actually work, and explains what [Unfolks](/) can say after a refresh — including the word "may", which is doing real work in that sentence.

## "Unfollowed you" is not "doesn't follow you back"

These get searched as if they were the same list. They are not.

- **Doesn't follow you back.** You follow them. They do not follow you. They might never have followed you. That list is a comparison of your following against your followers, and you can produce it today even if you have never checked before. The method is in [how to see who doesn't follow you back](/blog/how-to-see-who-doesnt-follow-you-back-on-instagram).
- **Unfollowed you.** They followed you at some earlier time, and they do not follow you now. To know that, you need the earlier time. Without a snapshot from then, you cannot tell a person who left from a person who was never there.

Someone you follow who does not follow you back might have unfollowed you, or they might have ignored the follow from the start. The list of non-followers does not say which. Treating it as an unfollow list will have you "catching" people who never followed you at all.

The reverse is also easy to miss. Someone can unfollow you and still be someone you do not follow. They will not appear on a non-follower list, because that list only contains accounts you follow. They disappeared from your followers, and a non-follower tool is not watching that edge unless it compares follower snapshots.

## Why there is no official history

Instagram shows you the followers you have now. It does not owe you a changelog, and it does not provide one in the app. Third-party apps cannot reconstruct a history they never stored. If they say they can, they are either comparing snapshots they took while you used them, or they are showing you something else — usually the non-follower list — and calling it unfollows.

A honest check has only two ingredients:

1. A saved copy of your followers from an earlier moment.
2. A fresh copy from today.

The accounts in the old copy that are missing from the new one are the ones who unfollowed, or whose accounts were removed, in between. You cannot see which of those two it was from the lists alone. You also cannot see anyone who followed and unfollowed entirely inside the gap, if they were absent from both copies.

That is the whole method. Everything else is a way of storing the copies.

## Two copies you can actually keep

### Two data downloads

Instagram's "Download your information" export includes your followers and following at the moment Instagram builds the file. Request it once and keep the file. Request it again later and keep that file too. Compare the follower lists yourself, or drop the new file into a tool that can read it.

This is the cleanest record, because the file comes from Instagram and the comparison does not have to crawl the site. It is also the slowest, because each export has to be requested and you have to wait for it. The request steps are in [find non-followers with your data download](/blog/find-non-followers-with-instagram-data-download). That article is about non-followers inside one file. For unfollows, you need two files, taken on different days, and you compare followers to followers.

Keep the old zip. If you download the new one on top of the old folder and let it replace the files, you have deleted your only snapshot. The point of the exercise is that both copies exist at once.

### A scan today and a scan later

[Unfolks](/) can scan the account you are signed in to and keep the result on your computer. The next time you refresh, it tells you how many accounts may have unfollowed you since that last scan. "May" is intentional. A difference between two scans can be someone who unfollowed, an account that was deactivated or renamed, or a scan that did not see the same page because Instagram limited it partway through. The extension does not pretend those cases are the same event with a timestamp and a motive.

What it does not do: invent a history from before you installed it. Your first scan is a starting line, not a backlog. If you have never saved a snapshot, nobody can tell you who left in 2022.

The scan uses your existing browser session. It does not ask for your password, and the lists stay local. If Instagram limits the scan, your place is kept and you can resume, which matters here: a half-finished scan is a bad snapshot to compare against. Let it finish, or resume it, before you treat the difference as people who left.

The data-file check inside the panel is a snapshot too, with no requests sent to Instagram while you read it. It answers who does not follow you back inside that file. It is not, by itself, an unfollow history.

## A habit that makes the question answerable

If you care about this more than once, decide on a rhythm and keep the copies.

- Monthly is enough for almost everyone. Weekly creates more noise than insight, and it means more scans.
- Use the data download when you want the record without crawling Instagram. Use a scan when you also want to review non-followers and maybe unfollow.
- Don't run a huge unfollow batch in the same sitting as a "who left" check. They are different jobs, and the batch is the one that can get you limited. The pace is in [how to mass unfollow safely](/blog/how-to-mass-unfollow-on-instagram-safely).

When you do look, read the names before you react. People unfollow because they are cleaning their own list, because they made a new account, or because they do not use Instagram anymore. A small number of unfollows on a large account is normal. It is not a verdict on your last post.

## What you should ignore

Be skeptical of any of these:

- A count of "unfollowers this week" from a tool you installed today. It did not see last week.
- A service that asks for your Instagram password so it can "monitor unfollows 24/7". You do not need to give anyone your password to compare two lists you already have.
- Exact times ("they unfollowed you at 14:03") unless you personally captured both sides of that minute. A daily or monthly snapshot cannot support a timestamp.
- Screenshots of other people's accounts offered as proof of a method. Your account is the only one a snapshot of your account describes.

The Chrome Web Store listing for Unfolks shows 20,000 users and a rating of 4.3 out of 5 from 69 ratings. That is evidence that people use the extension. It is not evidence that anyone can see an unfollow that was never recorded.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I see everyone who has ever unfollowed me?

No. Only the gap between snapshots you actually have. Anything before the first snapshot is unknowable from Instagram's current follower list.

### Does Instagram notify you at all?

Not with an unfollow alert. You may notice a lower follower count, and you may notice a specific person is gone if you remember them. The app does not name them for you.

### Why does Unfolks say "may have unfollowed"?

Because two scans can differ for more than one reason. Someone unfollowed, an account disappeared, or the earlier scan was incomplete. The extension reports the size of the change instead of inventing a certainty it does not have.

### If I unfollow people, will that show me who unfollowed me?

No. Unfollowing changes who you follow. It does not reveal who left your followers. It can also get the account limited if you do it in a rush. See the [limits guide](/blog/instagram-follow-and-unfollow-limits) before you turn a curiosity into a big batch.

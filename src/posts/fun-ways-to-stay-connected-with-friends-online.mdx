Social media makes it easy to *see* what your friends are up to — stories, posts, the occasional like. But scrolling past someone's photo isn't the same as actually spending time together. If most of your friendships live inside a feed, it's worth having a few go-to activities that turn passive following into real interaction.

Here are some simple, free ways to do something together online, even when you're in different cities or time zones.

### 1. Play a Quick Game Together

Nothing breaks the ice (or settles an argument) faster than a game. You don't need a console or a Discord server for this — some of the best options run right in the browser.

A favorite of ours: [Rock Paper Scissors Online](https://rock.paperscissors.online/), a free real-time version of the classic game. You create a room, send the link to a friend, and play best-of-three or best-of-five rounds live. No app install, no sign-up required, no ads. It's perfect for the small stuff friends argue about every day — who pays for coffee, who picks the movie, who texts the group first. Instead of going back and forth in the chat, just drop a game link and let fate decide. There's even a whole guide on [how to win rock paper scissors](https://rock.paperscissors.online/blog/how-to-win-rock-paper-scissors) if you want an unfair advantage backed by actual psychology.

### 2. Host a Watch Party

Most streaming platforms now support synchronized watching, and browser extensions fill the gaps for the rest. Pick a series, schedule a weekly episode, and keep a group chat open on the side. The show is almost secondary — the running commentary is where the fun lives.

### 3. Start a Shared Challenge

A step-count competition, a 30-day photo challenge, a "cook one new recipe a week" club. Shared challenges give you a reason to check in with each other that isn't just "hey, how's it going?" — and they generate exactly the kind of genuine content that makes social media fun again.

### 4. Have a Standing Call — with an Activity

Video calls where two people stare at each other run out of steam fast. Video calls where you're doing something — playing a quick browser game, doing a crossword, touring each other's neighborhoods on a walk — can go on for hours. The activity takes the pressure off the conversation.

### 5. Curate Each Other's Feeds

Send each other the best things you find online — not just memes, but accounts worth following, creators worth supporting. Being each other's algorithm is a surprisingly personal gesture, and it keeps your own feed fresh.

### Keep Your Online Circle Genuine

Staying connected also means knowing *who* you're actually connected to. If your Instagram following list has grown into a crowd of accounts that never engage back, it gets harder to see the people who matter. That's exactly what [Unfolks](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/idgjpjkoddplmbdepekendpdbaibcgpc) helps with — it shows you who doesn't follow you back so you can keep your circle real, and spend your online time on the friendships that go both ways.

Real connection online isn't about more followers or more scrolling. It's about shared moments — even one as small as a best-of-five rock paper scissors match.

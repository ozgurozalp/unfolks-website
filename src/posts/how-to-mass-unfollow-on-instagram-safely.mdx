Unfollowing one account is nothing. Unfollowing four hundred in a few minutes is a different action, and it is the one Instagram interrupts. People still want to do it, because a following list collected over years is full of accounts they do not remember and do not want. The safe version of a mass unfollow is slower, interruptible, and honest about the fact that Instagram never published the exact line you are not supposed to cross.

This is that version. It assumes you already know who does not follow you back, or you are about to find out. If you do not have the list yet, start with [how to see who doesn't follow you back on Instagram](/blog/how-to-see-who-doesnt-follow-you-back-on-instagram). The tool used in the steps below is [Unfolks](/), a free Chrome, Edge, and Brave side-panel extension. It uses the Instagram session already open in your browser. It does not ask for your password, and the lists stay on your computer.

No tool can guarantee an account will not be restricted. That includes Unfolks. What you can do is move at a pace Instagram is less likely to treat as a script, and stop the moment Instagram says stop.

## What "mass unfollow" actually does

Each unfollow is a normal Instagram action: you stop following one account. Doing them in a batch only means a tool sends those actions for you, with waits in between, instead of you tapping each profile. Instagram still sees a series of unfollows from your account. A browser extension does not get a special permit. A console script does not either. A website that asks for your password is not safer. It is just holding your login as well.

Speed matters more than the brand name on the tool. A batch that fires as fast as the page will respond looks nothing like a person. The list matters too. Unfollowing every non-follower is rarely what you want. Your Following list includes writers, shops, and friends you like who happen not to follow you. A mass unfollow should start shorter than the scan.

## Why accounts get limited, without a magic number

Instagram does not publish a follow limit or an unfollow limit. Posts that quote a precise daily cap are guessing, and the guess goes stale. The limit is not one number for every account. A new account, an account that was limited last week, and an account that has followed and unfollowed in loops for months are not treated the same. Instagram can also change the threshold without announcing it.

What you can observe, without inventing a figure, is the shape of a limit:

- Unfollows start failing, or Instagram tells you to try again later.
- The block is often temporary. Sometimes it lasts longer.
- Pushing more actions after the warning tends to make the wait worse, not better.
- Follows can be limited too. A follow/unfollow loop, the old growth trick of following a crowd and dropping them a day later, is a pattern Instagram has pushed back on for years.

What not to believe, and how a scan can be limited separately from an unfollow, is in the [Instagram follow and unfollow limits guide](/blog/instagram-follow-and-unfollow-limits).

## A pace that is built to be boring

[Unfolks](/) unfollows at a deliberate default:

- 4 to 6 seconds between unfollows.
- A 3 minute rest after every 5 unfollows.
- Both numbers can be changed in Settings. Making the waits longer is the safer direction.

That will not clear a thousand accounts during a coffee. A few hundred is an afternoon you can walk away from. A tool that advertises an instant wipe is advertising the behavior that gets accounts limited.

## Do this in order

### 1. Get a list you trust

Run **Show unfollowers** from the side panel while you are signed in on instagram.com, or use the data-file check if you want the look without sending Instagram a scan. The data-file screen cannot unfollow. It only shows you the names. To unfollow, you either open each profile yourself or run a scan and unfollow from that list. The file itself is covered in [using Download your information to find non-followers](/blog/find-non-followers-with-instagram-data-download).

### 2. Take people off the list before you select anyone

Hide every account you want to keep. Hidden accounts are left out of a bulk unfollow until you unhide them. Then use search, and the filters for verified, not verified, private, and no profile photo, to see the list in smaller pieces. Sort by when you followed them if you want to drop the oldest first.

Do this before Select all. Select all means the accounts still visible, and "still visible" should already be the ones you mean to leave.

### 3. Select a batch you could stop

Double-click an account to start selecting, or use Select all, then unfollow the selection. You can stop the batch while it is running. You can also copy the selected usernames if you want a record outside the extension.

Start smaller than you think. Fifty careful unfollows teach you whether Instagram is happy today. Four hundred teach you nothing if the account is stopped at sixty.

### 4. Leave it alone when Instagram says no

If Instagram starts refusing unfollows, Unfolks stops on its own and shows when you can try again. It does not keep clicking through the error. Wait. Do not switch to another tool and continue the same job. That is the same account doing the same action.

Short interruptions during a scan are waited out automatically. Longer ones tell you how long to wait, and the scan resumes from the account it stopped on instead of from the top. The progress of a stopped scan is kept for up to 24 hours. Large accounts can also pick a balanced or careful scan speed in Settings. Those speeds are about reading the lists, not about unfollowing faster.

## What "safe" does not mean

Safe does not mean undetectable, and it does not mean allowed. You are using your own account to unfollow accounts you follow. Instagram permits that. Instagram also limits bursts of it. An extension that waits is still automating taps you could have done yourself.

A few promises you should not accept from any tool, including ours:

- "You can't be banned if you use this." Nobody can know that. Instagram decides.
- "This many unfollows per hour is officially safe." Instagram has not published that number.
- "We unfollow from our servers, so your account is fine." Handing your session to someone else's server is a worse trade, not a better one.

The data-file check is the one path that does not ask Instagram for anything while you look. Use it when you only want the names. Use a slow batch only when you have decided to unfollow and you can leave the tab open.

## If you were already limited

Stop. The limit is information. Wait until Instagram lets you unfollow a single account by hand. Then, if you still want to continue, use a slower pace than the one that was stopped, and a smaller batch. Resuming a scan is fine once Instagram is answering again. Resuming a unfollow batch in the same hour it was blocked is how a short limit becomes a long one.

Nothing in Unfolks clears a limit. Its job after one is to wait and keep your place.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I unfollow everyone who doesn't follow me in one click?

You can select them and start a batch. You should not. Hide the accounts you mean to keep, select a portion, and let the waits run. One click that dumps the entire list at full speed is the outcome to avoid.

### What is the safest speed?

Instagram does not publish one. Unfolks defaults to 4–6 seconds between unfollows and a 3 minute rest after every 5, and you can slow both in Settings. Slower is the direction that reduces risk. It is not a guarantee.

### Will I lose followers if I unfollow people?

Unfollowing removes you from their followers, if you were one. It does not remove them from your followers. They were not following you. Your follower count changes only if someone who follows you decides to leave, which is a separate event.

### Can I undo a batch?

Instagram has no bulk "follow them all again" for an unfollow batch. If you dropped someone by mistake, follow them back from their profile. This is why hiding accounts before you start matters more than a clever undo button.
